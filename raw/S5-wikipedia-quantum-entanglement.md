## Definition
- Entanglement: the quantum state of each particle in a group cannot be described independently of the others, even when far apart. Mathematically, the state cannot be factored as a product of states of its local constituents; if it can, the state is separable.

## Bell state example
- (1/sqrt2)(|0>A|1>B - |1>A|0>B) is one of four Bell states: maximally entangled pure states forming an orthonormal basis of the two-qubit Hilbert space. Neither subsystem has a definite pure state on its own.

## Correlation under measurement
- If Alice measures in {|0>,|1>}, each outcome is equally likely; she can then predict Bob's result with certainty (here perfectly anti-correlated), even if spatially separated.

## No faster-than-light communication
- Entanglement produces correlations but cannot be used for faster-than-light communication: Alice's outcome is random and she cannot choose it, so she cannot transmit information to Bob (no-communication theorem).

## Uses in quantum computing
- Entangled states are a resource enabling communication and computation tasks. "Entanglement is necessary to realize quantum computing" – though disputed by some. Central to quantum teleportation and superdense coding.
